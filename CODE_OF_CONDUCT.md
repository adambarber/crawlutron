# Code of Conduct

## The short version

Be direct, be specific, and argue about the code. Treat the people you are
talking to as competent adults who are trying to make the same thing better.

## What we expect

- **Review the change, not the contributor.** A pull request is a proposal.
  "The gate reports two clones here" is a useful comment; "you clearly
  did not read the module" is not.
- **Say what you mean, and say why.** The bar in this repository is a
  reason, not a preference. If a comment cannot explain its own
  reasoning, it is not review yet.
- **Separate the finding from the fix.** Reporting a defect and proposing
  a patch are two different contributions, and a report is a complete
  contribution on its own.
- **Assume the other side has context you do not.** A one-line change
  that looks wrong is usually sitting on an invariant five files away.
  Ask before rewriting it.

## What we do not tolerate

- Harassment, personal attacks, or insults directed at a person rather
  than at their argument.
- Sustained disruption, or posting someone's private information.
- Sexualised language or imagery, and unwelcome sexual attention.
- Discriminatory or demeaning comments about who someone is or what they
  look like.

## Scope

This applies in every project space: issues, pull requests, review
threads, commit messages, and any channel where someone is representing
the project. It also applies to code you contribute — a payload, a
fixture, or a test string that demeans someone is still a contribution.

## Reporting

Report a problem through a private security advisory on GitHub, the same channel
`SECURITY.md` uses for vulnerability reports. Include the issue or pull
request, what happened, and roughly when. Reports are handled
confidentially, and a reporter who wants anonymity is not named in the
release notes.

A maintainer who does not follow this document is held to it the same
way anyone else is. Report that the same way you would report anything
else.

## Enforcement

A maintainer who sees conduct that does not fit this document may issue
a private correction, remove a comment or a commit, or close a thread.
The escalation, in order: a private note from a maintainer, then a
temporary block, then a permanent one. The choice and its severity follow
the conduct, not the volume of it.

## Why this is short

This is a small project maintained in public, and a longer document
mostly describes rules nobody would enforce. The two things that actually
matter are the first two sentences: review the change rather than the
person, and bring a reason.
